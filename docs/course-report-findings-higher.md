## Advice to teachers (appears in both years — the strongest signal)

These are the report's own closing recommendations, so they reflect what
markers saw repeatedly across the whole paper, not one question.

| Theme | Report wording (condensed) |
|---|---|
| Numerical skills | Practise basic numerical skills regularly, **particularly fractions and negative numbers** |
| Final answers | Check final answers carefully and **simplify** where appropriate |
| Working | Lay out working logically; **each line should follow from the line above** — "particularly important when differentiating and integrating, and when working with logarithms and exponentials" |
| Notation | Use notation and symbols accurately, **for example integral notation** |
| Brackets | Use brackets appropriately, particularly when finding the **area between curves** (2024) / calculating **definite integrals** (2025), and when **substituting negative numbers** into formulae |
| Trig | Practise **radian measure and the exact values** of trigonometric ratios |
| Vectors | Practise finding an **internal division point** of a line segment in three dimensions (2024) |
| Algorithms | Intervening steps matter — when factorising a quadratic, lines that are **inconsistent from line to line** do not gain full marks |
| Calculator | Use a calculator **efficiently** in paper 2 |
| Presentation | **Score out** working that is not part of the final response |
| Graphs | Practise **sketching graphs on provided axes** |

## By topic

### Straight line
- 2025: a few candidates stated an incorrect perpendicular gradient, which did not gain full marks.
- 2025: candidates **scored out terms in simultaneous equations**, leading to inconsistent lines of working; many used **elimination rather than substitution**, which led to errors.
- 2025 (collinearity): *"Few candidates communicated their conclusions unambiguously."*
- 2024: internal division point in 3D flagged in the advice section.

### Circles
- 2024 P1 Q13(b): few gained full marks — *"Most candidates did not use their answer to part (a) to find the centre of the circle."*
- 2024 P1 Q7: a few only found the centre and radius, *"which was not required"*.
- 2025: a few **stated the centre and radius with no other working**.
- 2025: determining a third circle's equation was answered well by only a few, and required communicating the approach.

### Discriminant and quadratics
- **2024 and 2025, near-identical wording**: most used the correct strategy, but some *"used an incorrect condition or did not state the inequality that they were solving."*
- 2025: marks lost by **not using brackets when substituting into b² − 4ac**.
- 2025: a few **used a single inequality to represent two regions** in the final answer.

### Polynomials
- 2025: a few presented the **synthetic division algorithm in a non-standard way**.
- 2025: some did not expand brackets or simplify accurately; **many did not extract a common factor**, making factorisation harder than necessary.
- 2024 P2 Q13: *"Many candidates made errors with signs."* Some had **no valid strategy for finding k**.
- 2024 P1 Q10: a few **did not communicate their conclusion**.

### Differentiation
- 2024 P2 Q2 and 2025: candidates **did not differentiate negative indices correctly**.
- 2025 (nature table): responses did not meet **minimum requirements for a nature table** — numerical errors evaluating the derivative near the stationary point, and **incorrect labelling** within the table.
- 2024 P2 Q9(b): *"Most candidates did not consider both ends of the closed interval."*
- 2024 P2 Q4(b) / 2025: sketching the derived function — **many did not draw a cubic**; most gained no marks. 2025 adds that some **treated it as a differential equation**.

### Integration
- 2025: some **omitted the constant of integration**, "which led to invalid working"; also failures to deal with the **coefficient of x**.
- 2025: **dx missing** from the statement of a definite integral; a few **switched the limits** and mishandled the resulting negative.
- 2024 P2 Q7 (area between curves): **errors with brackets**, and inefficient calculator use for substitution.
- 2024 P2 Q5: some **incorrectly evaluated a term as zero**.

### Trigonometry
- 2025: **worked in degrees throughout**, and a few **mixed degrees and radians in single lines of working**. Only a few dealt with the **phase change** accurately.
- 2025: **many did not extract a common factor** from a trig equation; a few **divided by cos x, which lost solutions**.
- 2024 P1 Q11(b) (sketching): *"Most candidates did not gain any marks."* A few **translated the graph in the wrong direction**; only a few **considered the height of the graph at both ends of the domain**.

### Logarithms and exponentials
- 2024 P2 Q11(b) and 2025: failure to **convert between exponential and logarithmic form** (both directions appear).
- 2025: **inconsistent lines of working** when converting.
- 2024 P2 Q6: some **used a relationship of the wrong form** when confirming a law from a straight-line graph.

### Vectors
- 2025: *"Many candidates did not gain any marks for this question."* Some **stated incorrect pathways**; some **did not add vectors given in i, j, k form**.

### Functions
- 2024 P2 Q8(b): *"Few candidates gave the condition for the function to be undefined."*
- 2024 P2 Q4(a): some did not clearly identify **which coordinates were the maximum point** of the transformed graph.
